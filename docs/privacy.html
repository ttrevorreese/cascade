Cascade privacy policy

Effective 25 October 2026 · Cascade for Android, by Trevor Reese

The short version: Cascade collects nothing. It has no account, no analytics, no ads and no servers, and Android does not even give it permission to use the internet. Everything you set up stays on your phone.

What Cascade keeps, and where

Cascade stores what it needs to ring your alarms in its own private storage on your phone, where other apps cannot read it:

  • Your alarm groups, alarms, labels, challenges and settings.
  • A log of what your alarms did (rang, snoozed, dismissed, missed), used for the Reliability Check and Stats.
  • Reference photos you take for the photo-match challenge, and audio files you choose as alarm sounds (Cascade keeps its own copies).
  • Spoken versions of alarm labels, if you turn on read aloud.

None of this is sent anywhere. Cascade turns off Android's cloud backup for its data, so it is not copied to Google Drive either. Uninstalling Cascade deletes all of it.

Permissions and why

  • Alarms and reminders, full-screen alarms, notifications: to ring on time and show the alarm over the lock screen.
  • Camera (only if you use those challenges): to scan a QR code or barcode, or to compare a photo with your reference photo. Images are processed on the phone and are not saved, apart from reference photos you choose to take.
  • Physical activity (only if you use the walking challenge): to count steps while the challenge is open. Step counts are not stored.
  • Run at startup: to set your alarms again after the phone restarts.

Cascade does not have the internet permission. Some Google libraries it uses (the on-device barcode reader and Google Play Billing) try to send anonymous usage statistics to Google; without that permission Android blocks them.

Purchases

Cascade is free and has no purchases at the moment. If Cascade Plus (extra themes) is offered later, it is bought through Google Play. Google handles the payment under Google's privacy policy. Cascade only learns whether you own Cascade Plus, from the Play Store app on your phone; it never sees your payment details.

Voices

Read aloud uses your phone's own text-to-speech engine and only voices that work offline. The engine is part of Android or comes from your phone's maker, and runs on the phone.

Spotify (test builds only)

Versions of Cascade distributed to a small group of testers can play a Spotify playlist as an alarm. Cascade then asks the Spotify app on your phone to play it; Cascade does not connect to Spotify's servers or see your Spotify account. Spotify's use of your data is covered by Spotify's privacy policy. The version on Google Play for everyone does not include this.

Exporting your data

Settings > Export everything saves your groups, alarms and event log as a file in a place you choose. What happens to that file afterwards is up to you.

Sending feedback

Settings > Feedback opens your own email app with a message addressed to the developer. It includes Cascade's version, your Android version and your phone's model, which help with problem reports; you can read, change or delete any of it before sending, and nothing is sent unless you send it. Cascade itself sends nothing. Emails you send are only used to reply to you and to improve Cascade.

Children

Cascade is not directed at children and collects no information from anyone.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be published at this address with a new effective date. Cascade will never start collecting personal data without saying so here first.
